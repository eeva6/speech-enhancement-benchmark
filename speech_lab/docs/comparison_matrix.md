# Comparison Matrix: Speech Enhancement Approaches

Companion to `literature_review.md` (see it for citations and verification notes). Latency means *algorithmic* latency (frame + lookahead) unless noted. "(paper)" means the figure comes from the cited paper. Any other figure is a typical value; re-measure it in this lab. CPU real-time means one consumer laptop core or a few.

Latency reference points:
- **DNS telephony budget:** ≤40 ms.
- **Occluding hearing aid:** disturbance above about 20 ms (Stone & Moore 1999).
- **Open-fit hearing aid:** about 5–6 ms just acceptable (Stone et al. 2008).

| Approach | Mechanism | Inputs | Complexity | Typical latency | Real-time on CPU? | Strengths | Weaknesses | Evidence limits | Representative code |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spectral subtraction (Boll; Berouti) | Subtract estimated noise magnitude per STFT bin, with over-subtraction α and floor β; noisy phase kept | 1 mic, noise estimate (VAD or leading silence) | FFT + O(K)/frame; negligible | 1 frame + hop, ≈20–32 ms (shorter frames possible) | Yes, trivially (MCU-class) | Simple, transparent, cheap | Musical noise; speech distortion; fails on non-stationary noise and babble | No significant intelligibility gain for NH listeners (Hu & Loizou 2007) | `pyroomacoustics.denoise` (https://github.com/LCAV/pyroomacoustics) |
| Wiener / MMSE-STSA / LSA with decision-directed a priori SNR | Per-bin statistical gain from a priori and a posteriori SNR; decision-directed smoothing | 1 mic + noise PSD tracker | FFT + O(K)/frame; ≪1% core | ≈20–32 ms (frame-bound) | Yes, trivially | Low musical noise; principled; industry standard in HAs and phones | Stationarity/Gaussian assumptions; onset lag smears consonants; babble | Improves quality and comfort, rarely intelligibility (Hu & Loizou 2007; Kim et al. 2009) | No canonical repo; implement from Ephraim–Malah / Scalart (lab baseline) |
| Noise PSD tracking (Martin min-stats; Cohen IMCRA) | Minimum of smoothed periodogram + bias correction; speech-presence-gated recursive averaging | 1 mic | O(K)/frame + 1–2 s buffer | Adds no frame latency; tracking lag ≈ search window (~1–2 s) | Yes | No VAD needed; robust to slowly varying noise | Slow to rising noise; speech-like noise leaks into estimate | Evaluated as part of full enhancers; component-level human evidence is minimal | Implement from papers (no verified official repo) |
| Adaptive noise cancelling (LMS/NLMS, Widrow) | FIR filter adapted on a noise-reference channel predicts and subtracts correlated noise | ≥2 sensors with a noise-only reference | O(L) MAC/sample | ≈0 (sample-wise) | Yes (DSP-class) | Large gains with a clean reference; near-zero latency | Target leakage cancels speech; fails in diffuse or reverberant fields | Strong for echo or engine noise with a reference; not applicable to the typical single-mic case | Textbook; no single canonical repo |
| SEGAN | Waveform U-Net GAN + L1 | 1 mic, 16 kHz | Large conv net; GPU-oriented | Non-causal (~1 s windows) | Not as published | End-to-end waveform; historically influential | Outperformed by later models; GAN artefacts | Objective metrics on VoiceBank-DEMAND only | https://github.com/santi-pdp/segan |
| Demucs denoiser (Défossez 2020) | Causal conv encoder–LSTM–decoder on waveform; multi-resolution STFT loss | 1 mic, 16 kHz | ~tens of M params; RTF 0.6 (H=48) to 1.05 (H=64) on laptop CPU (paper) | 40 ms incl. 3 ms lookahead, 16 ms stride (paper) | Borderline–yes (H=48) | Handles reverb and non-stationary noise; good waveform fidelity | Heavier than mask models; generalisation to real mics varies | MOS + objective metrics; no human word-recognition test | https://github.com/facebookresearch/denoiser |
| MetricGAN+ | BLSTM mask; discriminator learns PESQ, generator maximises it | 1 mic, 16 kHz | Small–medium BLSTM | Bidirectional, non-causal | Offline only (BLSTM) | High PESQ (3.15 VB-DEMAND, paper) | Metric over-fitting to PESQ; PESQ ≠ intelligibility | Quality-metric optimisation; no intelligibility evidence | https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/metricgan-plus-voicebank |
| RNNoise (Valin 2018) | GRU predicts 22 band gains + pitch comb filter | 1 mic, 48 kHz | ~40 MFLOPS; ~1.3% of one x86 core (paper) | 20 ms window, 10 ms hop (paper) | Yes, very cheap (embedded-class) | Tiny, production-proven, full-band | Coarse bands; limited on babble; attenuates speech at low SNR | MOS/PESQ-style evaluation; no intelligibility study | https://github.com/xiph/rnnoise |
| DeepFilterNet 1/2/3 | ERB gains + complex multi-frame deep filter (<~5 kHz) | 1 mic, 48 kHz | Low; RTF 0.04 (DFN2) / 0.19 single-thread (DFN3) on i5 (paper) | 40 ms (20 ms win, 10 ms hop, 2-frame lookahead) (paper) | Yes | Best open quality/complexity trade-off; Rust real-time path | 40 ms too long for open-fit HAs; residual artefacts at very low SNR | DNSMOS/PESQ/STOI on DNS & VB-DEMAND; no human intelligibility test | https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet |
| DCCRN | Complex conv-recurrent U-Net; complex ratio mask | 1 mic, 16 kHz | 3.7 M params (paper) | 37.5 ms semi-causal (paper) | Yes (DNS RT track #1) | Phase-aware; compact | Lookahead needed for best results | DNS subjective MOS; no intelligibility test | https://github.com/huyanxin/DeepComplexCRN |
| FullSubNet / FullSubNet+ | Full-band LSTM context + per-sub-band LSTM; cIRM | 1 mic, 16 kHz | Moderate; sub-band LSTM per frequency | ≈32 ms frame + small future-frame delay (paper) | Yes (meets DNS RT) | Strong stationarity modelling; competitive DNS results | Per-band LSTM cost scales with K | Objective DNS metrics only | https://github.com/Audio-WestlakeU/FullSubNet |
| Conv-TasNet | Learned encoder + dilated TCN masks + decoder, PIT | 1 mic, 8 kHz (benchmarks) | ~5 M params; GPU-oriented | Can be very low (~2 ms window) causal; best results non-causal | Causal variant feasible; quality drops | Handles speech-on-speech; low-latency capable | Unordered outputs; drops on noisy/reverberant/real data | 15.3 dB SI-SNRi on WSJ0-2mix (paper); minimal human testing | https://github.com/kaituoxu/Conv-TasNet (unofficial); Asteroid |
| DPRNN | Chunked intra/inter-chunk RNN over learned features | 1 mic | ~20× smaller than prior SOTA (paper) | Non-causal as published | Offline (causal variants possible) | Efficient long-sequence modelling | Same separation caveats | Synthetic mixtures only | https://github.com/asteroid-team/asteroid |
| SepFormer | Dual-path transformer masks | 1 mic, 8 kHz | ~26 M params; attention cost | Non-causal | No (GPU, offline) | 22.3 dB SI-SNRi WSJ0-2mix (paper) | Heavy; domain gap to real audio | SI-SNRi on synthetic data | https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/sepformer-wham · https://github.com/speechbrain/speechbrain |
| Delay-and-sum beamformer | Time-align and average channels toward target | ≥2 synced mics + geometry/DOA | O(MK)/frame or O(M)/sample | ≈0 (time-domain) to 1 frame | Yes | Linear, distortion-free; robust | Low gain vs diffuse noise at low freq for small arrays | Gain set by physics (≈10log10 M vs white noise); human benefit depends on placement | https://github.com/LCAV/pyroomacoustics |
| MVDR | Minimise output power, distortionless toward target RTF/steering vector | ≥2 mics + noise covariance + RTF/DOA | M×M inverse per bin; cheap for M≤8 | 1 STFT frame | Yes | Large SNR gains for directional interferers; linear | Target cancellation from steering/RTF errors and reverb | Mostly ASR (CHiME) and simulation | https://github.com/LCAV/pyroomacoustics · https://github.com/fgnt/pb_bss |
| GEV / max-SNR (+BAN post-filter) | Principal generalised eigenvector of Φ_xx, Φ_nn | ≥2 mics + speech/noise covariances | Eigen-decomposition per bin | 1 STFT frame | Yes | No geometry needed; max output SNR | Spectral colouring (needs post-filter); covariance estimation lag | ASR-centric evaluation | https://github.com/fgnt/pb_bss |
| Mask-based beamforming (Heymann 2016) | DNN T-F masks → covariance estimates → MVDR/GEV | ≥2 mics + trained mask net | Mask net + beamformer | Frame + covariance window (often utterance-level offline) | Online variants feasible | DNN robustness with linear-filter low distortion | Online covariance tracking is hard; array-specific | CHiME ASR WER; limited human intelligibility data | https://github.com/fgnt/pb_bss |
| Target-speaker extraction (SpeakerBeam, VoiceFilter, SpEx/SpEx+, WeSep) | Separation net conditioned on enrolment speaker embedding | 1 mic (or multi) + 3–10 s enrolment clip | Separation net + speaker encoder (once) | Mostly non-causal; causal variants exist | Mostly offline | Picks *the* talker; any number of interferers | Target confusion/switching (worse than no processing); enrolment logistics | SI-SDRi on synthetic mixtures; no human intelligibility studies found | https://github.com/BUTSpeechFIT/speakerbeam · https://github.com/wenet-e2e/wesep · https://github.com/maum-ai/voicefilter (unofficial) |
| *Contrast:* Passive hearing protection | Physical attenuation (seal, mass, absorption) of sound reaching the ear | Device fit | None | 0 | n/a | Real reduction of acoustic exposure | Leaks; bone-conduction ceiling ≈40–60 dB (Berger 2003); attenuates speech too | Measured as insertion loss / REAT, not SNR | n/a |
| *Contrast:* Acoustic ANC (FxLMS) | Loudspeaker anti-noise cancels pressure in a small zone | Ref/error mics + speaker in cup/canal | O(L)/sample, very low-latency DSP | Must be ≪1 ms-class (causality) | Dedicated DSP | Real low-frequency SPL reduction at the ear | Mainly <~1 kHz; cancels speech too unless passed through | Measured as SPL reduction; not a speech-enhancement metric | n/a (Kuo & Morgan 1999 tutorial) |

**Reading the matrix.**
- Only rows producing a signal that *replaces* what reaches the eardrum can deliver their output-SNR gain to a listener.
- The last two rows physically reduce sound at the ear. They are complementary to signal enhancement, not interchangeable with it.
- No neural single-channel row has a verified human-intelligibility result for the open model as released. The positive human evidence is from lab DNN maskers (Healy et al. 2013/2015/2019; Kim et al. 2009).
